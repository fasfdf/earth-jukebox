Earth Jukebox

Pick a record of our changing planet and listen to it. In the main list, every note is a real measurement.

Each stripe is one year of global temperature, 1880 to 2025, from NASA's record. Blue is cooler than average, red is warmer.

How to listen
  1. Choose a record, then press Play. Nothing plays until you do.
  2. Listen for pitch first: a higher note means a higher value. “Hear the reference notes” plays the lowest and highest notes, so you know the range before you start.
  3. Records under From NASA measurements turn the measured numbers into sound. Start with these.
  4. Records under From video colors turn the colors of a NASA animation into sound. They let you explore a visualization by ear, but color is only a picture of the data, not the measurement.

Keyboard: Tab moves between controls, Space plays or pauses, and the arrow keys move the timeline one step at a time when it has focus. On the measurement records, the step buttons let you explore at your own pace: each press plays one note and reads out its value. “Guided tour” stops the sound at each key moment so its description can be read or spoken without talking over the notes, then carries on. If you use a screen reader, leave “Read the tour aloud” off: your screen reader will read each description.

      Light to sound: try the math

      Light and sound are both waves, but visible light vibrates about a trillion times faster than anything we can hear. Halve a light frequency 40 times (drop it 40 octaves) and it lands in the range of a piano, with the spacing between colors kept intact.

      This is a way to explore the math, not a measurement. The swatch color is approximate.
